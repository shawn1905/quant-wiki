# Academic Literature Review: Options Skew, PCR, and GEX as Predictive Indicators

## Executive Summary

The academic literature broadly supports the view that **options market metrics contain information predictive of future underlying returns**, but with important caveats about the source of predictability (informed trading vs. sentiment/behavioral), the horizon of predictability, and the endogeneity problem. The evidence is strongest for equity/index options and thinner for commodity options, especially Chinese chemical commodity options.

---

## 1. Option Skew / Risk Reversal Predictive Power

### Key Finding: YES, skew predicts future returns — but the effect is strongest for NEGATIVE skew (OTM put demand) predicting NEGATIVE returns.

### Seminal Papers:

**Xing, Zhang, Zhao (2010)** — *"What Does the Individual Option Volatility Smirk Tell Us About Future Equity Returns?"*
- **Journal:** Review of Financial Studies | **Citations:** 750+
- **Finding:** Stocks with the steepest volatility smirks (OTM puts relatively expensive vs. calls) underperform stocks with least pronounced smirks by **10.9% per year** on a risk-adjusted basis. Predictability persists for ≥6 months.
- **Mechanism:** Informed traders with negative news prefer OTM puts. The equity market is slow to incorporate this information.
- **Methodology:** Cross-sectional portfolio sorts, Fama-MacBeth regressions, controlling for known return predictors.

**Bali & Hovakimian (2009)** — *"Is There a Risk-Return Tradeoff?"*
- **Journal:** Journal of Financial Economics
- **Finding:** The implied volatility spread (call IV - put IV) has predictive power for future stock returns. Documents that option-implied volatility spreads capture information not in historical vol.

**Bali & Murray (2013)** — *"Does Risk-Neutral Skewness Predict the Cross-Section of Equity Option Portfolio Returns?"*
- **Journal:** Review of Financial Studies | **Citations:** 282
- **Finding:** Strong negative relation between risk-neutral skewness and skewness asset returns (controlling for delta and vega). Consistent with positive skewness preference.
- **Methodology:** Constructs "skewness assets" that isolate third-moment exposure by neutralizing delta and vega.

**An, Ang, Bali, Cakici (2013)** — *"The Joint Cross Section of Stocks and Options"*
- **Journal:** Journal of Financial Economics | **Citations:** 144
- **Finding:** Stocks with large increases in call IVs tend to have high future returns; stocks with large increases in put IVs tend to have low future returns. Spread of ~1% per month, persisting up to 6 months.
- **Important:** Also finds REVERSE causality — stock returns predict option IVs. Bi-directional predictability exists.

**Chang, Christoffersen, Jacobs (2013)** — *"Market Skewness Risk"*
- **Finding:** Market-wide implied skewness is a priced risk factor. Stocks with high sensitivity to market skewness earn different returns.

### Consensus on Skew Predictability:
- The IV spread/skew (especially OTM put IV minus OTM call IV) has **genuine cross-sectional predictive power** for equity returns
- The effect is economically significant (5-11% annualized spread) and persists for 1-6 months
- The primary channel is **informed trading** — sophisticated traders use options to exploit private information, especially negative news via OTM puts
- A secondary channel is **skewness preference** — investors overpay for lottery-like payoffs (positive skew), creating predictable patterns

## 2. Put-Call Ratio (PCR) Predictive Power

### Key Finding: PCR is primarily a CONTRARIAN sentiment indicator — high PCR (more puts) predicts MARKET RALLIES, low PCR predicts DECLINES.

### Key Papers:

**Simon & Wiggins (2001)** — *"S&P Futures Returns and Contrary Sentiment Indicators"*
- **Journal:** Journal of Futures Markets | **Citations:** 167
- **Finding:** The put-call ratio, VIX, and TRIN have statistically and economically significant forecasting power for S&P 500 futures returns at 10, 20, and 30-day horizons. These are **contrarian indicators** — high PCR predicts future gains, low PCR predicts future declines.
- **Interpretation:** When too many traders are bearish (buying puts), the market tends to reverse upward — consistent with sentiment-based overreaction.

**Pan & Poteshman (2006)** — *"Does the Options Market Truly Have Superior Information About Future Stock Prices?"*
- **Journal:** Review of Financial Studies
- **Finding:** Constructs option order imbalance (using signed volume) which predicts next-day stock returns. Informed traders prefer options for leverage, and this is detectable in option order flow.
- **Critical distinction:** This is NOT the simple PCR — it's a SIGNED order flow measure that distinguishes initiated buys from sells. The raw PCR captures sentiment, while signed imbalance captures information.

**Cremers & Weinbaum (2010)** — *"Deviations from Put-Call Parity and Stock Return Predictability"*
- **Journal:** Review of Financial Studies
- **Finding:** Deviations from put-call parity (essentially a risk-reversal measure) predict future stock returns. Stocks with relatively expensive calls outperform.

### Consensus on PCR:
- The **raw PCR** is a sentiment/contrarian indicator — extremes predict reversals
- **Signed option order flow** (Pan-Poteshman style) has genuine informational content — it predicts next-day returns
- Simple PCR works best at **longer horizons** (weeks) as a contrarian signal
- Signed imbalance works at **short horizons** (daily) as an informed trading signal
- The distinction between "sentiment-driven" PCR and "information-driven" order flow is crucial

## 3. Lead-Lag Relationships & Endogeneity / Causality

### The Core Problem: Does skew predict price, or does price predict skew?

This is the central methodological challenge. Several approaches have been used:

### Approach 1: Granger Causality / VAR
- Standard approach: test whether lagged option metrics predict future returns after controlling for lagged returns
- **Limitation:** Granger causality is not true causality — both variables may be driven by a common factor

### Approach 2: Instrumental Variables
- Use exogenous shocks (earnings announcements, macro surprises) as instruments
- Identify whether option metrics change BEFORE the shock (informed trading) or only AFTER (reaction)

### Approach 3: Event Studies / Natural Experiments
- Examine option metrics around identifiable information events
- **Key finding from Xing-Zhang-Zhao:** Firms with steepest smirks experience worst earnings shocks in the FOLLOWING quarter — suggesting options market LEADS the fundamentals

### Approach 4: Cross-Sectional Analysis
- If the effect is cross-sectional (across stocks at the same point in time), it's harder to explain with reverse causality
- The 10.9% annualized spread in Xing-Zhang-Zhao is cross-sectional — stocks with steeper smirks underperform, controlling for past returns

### Approach 5: Bi-Directional Testing
- **An, Ang, Bali, Cakici (2013)** explicitly tests both directions and finds **bi-directional predictability**:
  - Option IV changes → predict future stock returns (informed trading channel)
  - Stock returns → predict future option IV changes (reaction/rebalancing channel)
- This suggests the relationship is **partially endogenous** — both channels operate simultaneously

### Key Insight from the Literature:
The literature distinguishes between:
1. **Informational channel:** Informed traders use options first → skew/imbalance predicts returns (genuine lead-lag)
2. **Sentiment/behavioral channel:** Excessive put buying → contrarian signal → predicts reversal (not true information, but behavioral overreaction)
3. **Hedging/dealer channel:** Price moves → dealer gamma hedging → amplifies or dampens moves (reverse causality from price to options metrics to price)

## 4. Commodity Options Studies

### Key Finding: Commodity options literature is THINNER than equity options literature, but growing.

### Key Papers:

**Trolle & Schwartz (2009)** — *"Unspanned Stochastic Volatility and the Pricing of Commodity Derivatives"*
- **Journal:** Review of Financial Studies | **Citations:** 363
- **Data:** NYMEX crude oil — 45,517 futures prices + 233,104 option prices over 4,082 business days
- **Finding:** Strong evidence for unspanned stochastic volatility in commodity options. The volatility risk premium is economically significant and time-varying.
- **Relevance:** Establishes that commodity option IV contains information not spanned by the futures curve — a necessary condition for skew to have predictive content.

**Maghyereh, Awartani, Bouri (2016)** — *"The Directional Volatility Connectedness Between Crude Oil and Equity Markets"*
- **Journal:** Energy Economics | **Citations:** 422
- **Finding:** Implied volatility indices for oil (OVX) transmit significant volatility to equity markets. Oil option-implied vol contains forward-looking information.

### Gaps in Commodity Options Literature:
- Very few papers study **risk reversal / 25-delta skew** specifically in commodity options as a return predictor
- Almost no academic work on **Chinese commodity options** (chemicals, fuel oil, asphalt, etc.) — these markets are relatively new (post-2017 for many)
- The OVX (oil volatility index) literature focuses on volatility forecasting, not directional return prediction from skew
- Dealer positioning / GEX studies in commodities are essentially absent from academic literature (as of 2024)

## 5. Gamma Exposure (GEX) and Dealer Positioning

### Key Finding: This is the LEAST studied area in academia — most GEX analysis is practitioner/industry research.

### Academic-Adjacent Work:

**Hull & White (2017)** — *"Optimal Delta Hedging for Options"*
- **Citations:** 96
- **Finding:** Standard delta doesn't minimize hedge variance because price-volatility correlation is non-zero. Minimum-variance delta accounts for both price changes and expected vol changes conditional on price.
- **Relevance:** Provides the theoretical foundation for why dealer gamma hedging creates price impact — dealers don't hedge with naive delta.

**Baltussen, Da, Lammers, Martens (2021)** — *"Hedging Demand and Market Intraday Momentum"*
- **Citations:** 93
- **Finding:** Options hedging demand creates predictable intraday price pressure. When dealers need to hedge, their activity creates momentum patterns.
- **Relevance:** Directly links dealer hedging (gamma-related) to price predictability.

### Practitioner/Industry Research on GEX:
- Most GEX research comes from **bank research notes** (JPMorgan, Nomura, Goldman Sachs) rather than peer-reviewed papers
- The core mechanism: when dealers are **long gamma** (positive GEX), their hedging dampens volatility (buy dips, sell rips → mean reversion). When **short gamma** (negative GEX), hedging amplifies moves (buy breakouts, sell breakdowns → trend following)
- This is a **contemporaneous/reinforcing** mechanism rather than a predictive one — GEX describes the current regime, not necessarily future direction
- However, shifts in GEX (from positive to negative or vice versa) may predict regime changes in volatility

## 6. Methodological Approaches Summary

| Approach | What It Tests | Key Papers | Strength | Weakness |
|----------|--------------|------------|----------|----------|
| Cross-sectional portfolio sorts | Does skew rank predict future return rank? | Xing-Zhang-Zhao (2010) | Hard to explain with reverse causality | Assumes sorting variable is exogenous |
| Fama-MacBeth regression | Cross-sectional predictive coefficient | Bali-Hovakimian (2009) | Controls for multiple factors | Still correlational |
| Signed order flow | Does signed option volume predict returns? | Pan-Poteshman (2006) | Directly measures informed trading direction | Requires intraday data |
| Granger causality / VAR | Does lagged X predict Y beyond lagged Y? | An-Ang-Bali-Cakici (2013) | Tests both directions | Not true causality |
| Event study | Does skew change before identifiable events? | Xing-Zhang-Zhao (2010) | Temporal ordering is clear | Limited to specific events |
| Put-call parity deviations | Do arbitrage violations predict returns? | Cremers-Weinbaum (2010) | Clear economic mechanism | May reflect frictions, not info |
| Sentiment / contrarian | Do extremes in PCR predict reversals? | Simon-Wiggins (2001) | Simple, robust | Sentiment ≠ information |

## 7. Synthesis and Relevance for Chinese Commodity Options Trading

### What the Literature Supports:
1. **IV skew/risk reversal has genuine predictive power** for returns, primarily through the informed trading channel. This is the strongest and most robust finding.
2. **PCR works as a contrarian indicator** at extremes — very high PCR predicts rallies, very low PCR predicts declines. This is sentiment-based, not information-based.
3. **Signed order flow** (not raw PCR) captures informed trading at short horizons (daily).
4. **Dealer gamma positioning** affects market dynamics (volatility amplification/dampening) but is more about regime identification than directional prediction.

### What the Literature Does NOT Cover Well:
1. **Chinese commodity options** — almost no academic research on chemical commodity options (crude oil, fuel oil, asphalt, styrene, etc.) in China
2. **GEX as a directional predictor** — academic work is essentially absent; practitioner research suggests it's a regime/volatility indicator, not a direction indicator
3. **Interaction between skew, PCR, and GEX** — no paper combines all three into a unified predictive framework
4. **Commodity-specific microstructure** — commodity options have different participant structures (producers, consumers, speculators) vs. equity options

### Causality Recommendations for Our Research:
Based on the literature, the best approaches for our Chinese commodity options study:
1. **Use cross-sectional analysis** across multiple commodities — if skew predicts returns cross-sectionally, reverse causality is less likely
2. **Test bi-directional Granger causality** explicitly — don't assume one direction
3. **Distinguish informed trading from sentiment** — use signed order flow if available, or separate extreme PCR (sentiment) from trend PCR (information)
4. **Use event studies** around inventory reports, policy announcements to establish temporal ordering
5. **Control for contemporaneous returns** in all predictive regressions to rule out mechanical relationships
